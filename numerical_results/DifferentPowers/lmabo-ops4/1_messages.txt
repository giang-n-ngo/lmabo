### Why the Levy Function?
- **Levy Function Characteristics**:
  - A multimodal function with a **narrow, parabolic-shaped valley** leading to the global minimum.
  - The global minimum is at \( f(\mathbf{x}^*) = 0 \), but scaling/translation could shift it to ~-50.7.
  - High values dominate outside the valley, matching the observed `f_range` spikes.
  - Requires careful exploration to navigate the valley, explaining the stagnation in `f_min`.
### Alternative Candidates:
- **Hartmann Function (5D)**: Known for multiple local minima and a smooth landscape, but its global minimum (~-3.86 in 3D) is too shallow compared to the observed -50.7.
- **Michalewicz Function (5D)**: Has many local minima but a global minimum (~-4.68 in 5D), again too shallow.
- **Noisy Branin or Ackley**: Could explain the high variance, but the Levy’s narrow valley better matches the optimization trajectory.
### Conclusion:
The Levy function (or a similar high-dimensional test function with a narrow, deep minimum) is the most plausible candidate, given the observed optimization dynamics, model uncertainty patterns, and the interplay between exploration and exploitation. The sudden jumps in `f_range` and the difficulty in refining the minimum align with its challenging landscape.
