1.  **Multi-modality:** The most striking evidence is the prolonged periods of stagnation at a particular `f_min` (e.g., from N=11 to N=30, then N=36 to N=41) followed by sudden, significant improvements after an exploratory or information-gathering acquisition function (like `qKG`, `LogPI`, `TS` in earlier stages, or even `LogEI` and `EI` once a better region was found). This pattern strongly indicates the presence of multiple local optima, where the optimizer gets stuck until a broader search pushes it into a new, better basin of attraction.
2.  **Locally Smooth:** Once a new `f_min` was discovered, the model's lengthscales consistently became small and concentrated (especially from N=31 onwards), suggesting a confident, accurate local model within the vicinity of the current best. This allowed direct exploitation strategies (like `EI` and `LogEI`) to work effectively in finding follow-up improvements within that local basin.
3.  **Tendency for Over-exploitation:** The frequent and rapid drops in `shortest distance` to critically low values (e.g., 0.029, 0.0065, 0.026) immediately after an `f_min` improvement or an exploitation step highlight that the optimizer was quickly converging and clustering points very tightly around the perceived optimum within a basin. This necessitated frequent switches to exploration-promoting functions like `UCB` to move away from these tight clusters and continue progress.

This behavior is characteristic of many challenging benchmark functions like **Ackley**, **Rastrigin**, or **Schwefel**, which possess numerous local minima but are generally smooth within those basins, requiring a careful balance of global exploration and local exploitation to find the true global optimum.
