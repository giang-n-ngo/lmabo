1.  **Frequent Stagnation and Breakthroughs:** The `f_min` repeatedly gets stuck for several iterations (stagnation), only to eventually find a significantly better value (breakthrough). This episodic improvement is a strong indicator of a landscape with multiple local optima.
2.  **Alternating Exploitation and Exploration Strategies:** My recommendations consistently shifted between:
    *   **Exploitation-focused ACQs (LogEI, UCB, PI)** immediately after a new `f_min` or when lengthscales were tight, indicating the model had found a promising local region and was trying to refine it.
    *   **Exploration-focused ACQs (qPES, qMES, TS, PosSTD, qKG, qJES)** when `f_min` was stagnant, especially when lengthscales dramatically widened, signaling the model's loss of local confidence and a need for global re-evaluation.
3.  **Dynamic Lengthscales:** The lengthscales frequently transitioned from very tight and small (indicating precise local understanding) to very wide and variable (indicating global uncertainty and a need to explore across dimensions). This behavior is characteristic of a model grappling with multiple distinct regions of interest in a complex, high-dimensional space.
4.  **High Dimensionality (D=50):** In 50 dimensions, purely local search is very prone to getting trapped. The need for robust global exploration (entropy-based methods, Thompson Sampling) in such a space further supports the multimodal hypothesis.
5.  **Shortest Distance Behavior:** The `shortest_distance` frequently became very small after finding a new `f_min`, prompting the use of UCB to explicitly balance exploration and prevent over-exploitation in crowded, recently improved areas. This "trap-and-escape" cycle is typical of multimodal functions.

This dynamic perfectly matches the characteristics of a complex, multimodal landscape.
