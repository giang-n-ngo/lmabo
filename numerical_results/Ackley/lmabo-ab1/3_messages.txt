Based on the optimization history, several characteristics strongly point towards a challenging, multi-modal objective function, likely one of the standard benchmarks for high-dimensional optimization.

Here's a summary of the evidence:

1.  **High Dimensionality (D=50):** This immediately suggests a function designed to be difficult in many dimensions, where the curse of dimensionality is significant.
2.  **Repeated Stagnation and Sudden Drops in `f_min`:** The `f_min` remained stuck for many iterations, often for 5-10+ steps, before suddenly dropping to a new, better value. This pattern occurred repeatedly (e.g., from 20.855 to 20.380, then 20.084, 19.983, 19.745, 19.616, 19.569, 18.736, 18.353, 17.577, and finally 16.723). This behavior is characteristic of an optimizer repeatedly getting trapped in local minima and then eventually finding a path to a better one.
3.  **Fluctuating Lengthscales:** The model's lengthscales showed wide variations. They sometimes ranged very broadly (e.g., mean 213, max 513 or mean 2191, max 4237), indicating exploration in flat or uncertain regions. Other times, especially after finding new minima or when stalled, they became extremely tight (e.g., mean 1.3, max 4.053; mean 2.1, max 7.3), indicating the model was focusing very narrowly on sharp features or local optima. This oscillation is a hallmark of highly rugged, multimodal landscapes.
4.  **Shortest Distance Behavior:** After improvements in `f_min`, the `shortest distance` often became very small (e.g., 0.59, 0.34, 0.22, 0.38, 0.33, 0.11, 0.38), indicating aggressive local exploitation around new optima. This further supports the presence of sharp, distinct minima.
5.  **Acquisition Function Cycle:** The necessity to frequently cycle through various acquisition functions—including information-theoretic ones (qMES, qJES, qPES) to gain knowledge, balanced ones (TS, UCB) to avoid local traps, pure exploration (PosSTD) to escape, and aggressive exploitation (PI, LogEI, PosMean) to push for small gains—demonstrates the persistent challenge of the objective function.

Given these observations, especially the high dimensionality, the strong multimodal behavior, and the positive `f_min` values that are decreasing, I strongly suspect the objective function is a variant of the **Ackley function**.

The Ackley function is known for its many local minima, which are typically concentrated around a single, deep global minimum. Its characteristic "mountain ranges" and "valleys" align well with the observed stagnation in local optima and the subsequent drops to lower `f_min` values as the optimizer navigates to better regions. The typical range of values for an unscaled Ackley function also aligns with the observed `f_range` and `f_min` values (e.g., global minimum at 0, but many local minima with positive values).
