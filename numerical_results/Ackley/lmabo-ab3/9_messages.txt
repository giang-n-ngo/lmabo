Based on the optimization trajectory, the function being optimized most strongly resembles a **multimodal function with a high number of local minima**, which frequently trapped the optimizer. The significant drops in `f_min` (e.g., from ~20.9 to 20.5, then 20.3, then 20.2, then a massive jump to 18.7, then 18.0, and finally 17.4) were consistently preceded by acquisitions focused on global exploration (like **TS**, **PosSTD**) or information gain (like **qMES**, **qPES**, **qKG**), suggesting the need to escape local optima. Once a promising region was found, highly exploitative strategies like **PosMean** delivered consecutive improvements.

This pattern, especially the high dimensionality (D=50) combined with the need for strong exploratory functions to break out of prolonged stagnation periods, is highly characteristic of the **Ackley function**. The Ackley function is known for its "waves" that create a multitude of local minima, making it challenging for optimizers to converge to its global minimum without robust exploration. The specific `f_min` values observed would correspond to a shifted or scaled version of the standard Ackley function.
