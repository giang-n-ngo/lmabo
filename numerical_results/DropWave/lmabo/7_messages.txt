1.  **Initial Lengthscales & Shift:** The optimization started with very large and highly variable lengthscales (Range [0.124, 378.595], Mean 189.360). This quickly shifted to consistently *extremely small* lengthscales (e.g., mean 0.196, then 0.014, and frequently staying around 0.02-0.04). This behavior is characteristic of the Ackley function, which has a broad, relatively flat outer region (leading to large lengthscales initially) and a highly oscillatory, rugged central region containing the global minimum (leading to consistently small lengthscales once the model learns this structure).
2.  **Persistent Ruggedness:** For the vast majority of the optimization, the lengthscales remained very small, indicating a highly complex, rugged, and likely multi-modal landscape. This aligns perfectly with the dense local minima present in the Ackley function's oscillatory core.
3.  **Optimization Difficulty & Plateau:** The `f_min` improved relatively early but then plateaued for a very long time, requiring a prolonged phase of aggressive exploitation (using LogEI). This difficulty in finding further improvements is expected in a function like Ackley, where navigating the many local minima to reach the true global minimum is challenging.
4.  **2D Dimensionality:** The function is 2D, which is a common dimension for standard Ackley implementations.

While other functions like Rastrigin or Schwefel are also highly multi-modal and rugged, the distinct pattern of initial large lengthscales quickly transitioning to consistently very small ones strongly points towards the Ackley function's unique structure.
