Here's why:

1.  **Multimodality:** The most striking feature is the repeated pattern of stagnation in `f_min` (even with converged lengthscales), followed by a significant widening of lengthscales (indicating increased model uncertainty and a need for broader exploration), and then a subsequent improvement in `f_min` after exploration (often with Thompson Sampling) or after the lengthscales re-converge (with Expected Improvement). This strongly suggests the optimizer is repeatedly getting stuck in local minima and then jumping out to find better ones, which is characteristic of highly multimodal functions.
2.  **f_min Trajectory:** The `f_min` starts relatively high (around 54) and gradually decreases through several "plateaus" and "drops" to a final value of 3.902. Many benchmark multimodal functions have a global minimum of 0, and approaching this value in stages is typical.
3.  **Lengthscales Behavior:** The oscillation between very tight (e.g., mean ~1, std dev ~0.2) and very wide (e.g., mean ~5000, std dev ~3000) lengthscales is key. Tight lengthscales suggest the model has a very good local understanding (exploiting), while wide lengthscales indicate high uncertainty or a transition to a new, less understood region (exploring). This active adaptation to multimodal basins is a strong indicator.
4.  **f_range:** The observed `f_range` reaching up to 342.475 is consistent with the Rastrigin function for D=13 over typical search domains (e.g., [-5.12, 5.12]), where the function can indeed take on large values far from the global minimum of 0. The Ackley function, another highly multimodal candidate, typically has a much smaller maximum value (around 20-30).
5.  **LogEI Usage:** The use of LogEI towards the end of the optimization, when `f_min` is very low and lengthscales are extremely tight, suggests that the true global minimum is likely very close to 0, and the expected improvement becomes numerically very small, requiring the logarithmic scaling for stability and sensitivity to minute gains.

The Rastrigin function (D=13) perfectly embodies these characteristics: it is highly multimodal with many local optima, a global minimum at 0, and can have large function values over typical domains, explaining the wide `f_range` and the optimization's journey through various basins.
