Key characteristics that point to this:
1.  **Multimodality and Traps:** The function initially found a local minimum around -260, then jumped significantly to -856, then to -887. The most telling sign is the *extremely long plateau* at `f_min = -887.764` (from N=17 to N=37), during which various aggressive exploratory acquisition functions (TS, UCB, qMES, qPES, qJES) were unable to escape. This is a hallmark of Schwefel's function, which is notorious for having a large, deceptive local minimum that "traps" optimizers far from the true global minimum.
2.  **Small Final Improvement:** After the long trap, a very slight improvement to `f_min = -888.907` was achieved, followed by even smaller, incremental improvements. This is consistent with escaping a deep local minimum to find the true global minimum, which is often only slightly better than the deceptive local one in Schwefel's function.
3.  **Lengthscales:** The lengthscales became consistently small (around 0.06-0.07) after N=7, suggesting a complex, rugged, or highly varying landscape around the minima, which fits the nature of Schwefel's function.
4.  **Dimensionality:** D=2 is a common dimensionality for benchmarking Schwefel's function.

While the final `f_min` value of approximately -888.946 doesn't perfectly match the standard Schwefel global minimum of -D * 418.9829 = -837.9658 for D=2, benchmark functions are often modified with different scales or offsets. The *behavior* of the optimization, especially the difficulty in escaping the local minimum, is highly indicative of Schwefel's function.
