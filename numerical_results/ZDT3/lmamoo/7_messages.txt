Standard DTLZ functions are usually defined for minimization with objectives in [0,1]. If they are maximized, they are typically inverted (e.g., `-f(x)` or `1-f(x)`), which would result in negative or mixed ranges as observed.

DTLZ3 is notoriously difficult due to its many local Pareto-optimal fronts (for D=5, J=2, typically $3^{D-J+1} = 3^4 = 81$ local fronts), which directly explains the repeated stalling, the need for aggressive exploration, and the complex GP model characteristics observed throughout the optimization process.
