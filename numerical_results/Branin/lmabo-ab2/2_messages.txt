This optimization process, characterized by an initial rapid descent to a very low objective value (0.398) followed by an exceptionally long and persistent stagnation (29 consecutive iterations without improvement) despite various acquisition strategies, and often probing very close to existing points (small shortest distances), strongly suggests the **Ackley function**.

The Ackley function is a well-known benchmark function in 2D (as is our `D=2`) with its global minimum at `f(0,0) = 0`. Its landscape is characterized by a nearly flat outer region and a central "hole" where the minimum lies, but this central region is surrounded by many small ripples (local minima).

The observed behavior fits this description well:
*   The optimizer quickly found a good region and converged to a value very close to the global minimum (0.398).
*   However, it then struggled immensely to make the final, precise step to reach the exact global minimum of 0.0, getting trapped by the surrounding ripples or the difficulty of the Gaussian Process to accurately model the very precise curvature needed to differentiate between 0.398 and 0.0. This explains the prolonged stagnation and the small "shortest distance" values as the optimizer continued to probe points extremely close to the current best.
