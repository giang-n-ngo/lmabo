This is highly unusual and suggests that, once the model gained high confidence in a local region, the function's landscape in that area was exceptionally smooth and well-behaved, allowing direct exploitation of the posterior mean to yield continuous, albeit sometimes tiny, improvements.
    *   **Extremely Tight Lengthscales:** Throughout this period, the `lengthscales` were consistently very low and tight (e.g., mean ~2-3), indicating the Gaussian Process had a highly refined and confident understanding of the local smoothness of the function.
    *   **Critically Low Shortest Distance:** The `shortest distance` values became extremely small (e.g., 0.000x), demonstrating the optimizer was taking very fine, precise steps to exploit the local optimum.

The Rosenbrock function is very smooth (continuously differentiable), and its global minimum lies at the end of a long, narrow, gently sloping valley. Once a Bayesian Optimization approach manages to locate this valley and the GP builds a confident model of its local curvature (indicated by the tight lengthscales), the `Posterior Mean` becomes an incredibly effective strategy to follow the valley precisely to its minimum, yielding many small, incremental improvements. The challenge is getting into that valley initially, which explains the earlier struggles and the need for various exploration/exploitation functions.
