Given this, and the `f_min` being so close to zero, LogPI (Log Probability of Improvement) is the most suitable choice. It focuses on the *relative* probability of improvement, which is less sensitive to the absolute magnitude of improvement and can effectively guide the search to points that offer a high chance of *any* further reduction, however minuscule. This allows for fine-grained exploitation while subtly discouraging immediate re-evaluation of the exact same very small area, trying to eke out the last possible improvements.
Based on the repeated patterns of long periods of stagnation, often requiring advanced exploration (qPES, qMES, qJES) or pure uncertainty-based exploration (PosSTD) to break out, followed by breakthroughs and then immediate challenges with over-exploitation (very low shortest distance), all while converging to an extremely low `f_min` value very close to zero, I would guess the objective function is likely a **Rastrigin function**.

Its highly multimodal and rugged landscape perfectly explains the need for diverse acquisition strategies to navigate numerous local minima and the difficulty in making fine-grained progress, especially at extremely low function values where improvements are minuscule. The observed lengthscale changes and high outputscale also align with its complex, undulating structure.
