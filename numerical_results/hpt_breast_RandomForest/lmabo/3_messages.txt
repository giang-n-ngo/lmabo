qJES: `PI` failed to improve `f_min`. With only 3 iterations remaining and persistent `f_min` stagnation, combined with diverging lengthscales (range from ~233 to ~492, std dev from ~86 to ~226), the model is still highly unstable. In these critical final steps, instead of gambling on an immediate improvement with a shaky model, it's more strategic to invest in maximizing the information gain to ensure the *best possible model* is built, even if it doesn't yield an immediate `f_min` improvement. qJES (Joint Entropy Search) explicitly aims to reduce the entropy of the posterior distribution over the *entire function*. This provides the most robust global understanding possible within the remaining few iterations, which can lead to a more reliable prediction of the true optimum in the absolute final steps, or at least a clearer picture of the function landscape.

It focuses on maximizing the expected *future* improvement in the best function value by explicitly valuing information. Even with only two steps, qKG intelligently selects the point that will provide the most valuable information to refine the model for the *absolute final step*, aiming to enable the best possible final decision based on a more informed model. At the very end, we must commit to the point the model believes is currently the best, even if the model itself is not perfectly stable.
Based on the optimization history, particularly the following observations:

1.  **Extreme and Persistent Lengthscale Instability:** The lengthscales repeatedly diverged to very large values (thousands) and then converged (sometimes dramatically) to much smaller values (tens/hundreds). This indicates that the Gaussian Process model consistently struggled to accurately and stably capture the global structure, smoothness, and relevance of different dimensions of the objective function. This behavior is typical when the function has a highly complex, multi-modal, or deceptive landscape.
2.  **Stagnant `f_min`:** The best observed objective value (`f_min`) improved only once (from -0.932 to -0.941) early in the process (N=14) and then remained stuck at -0.941 for the vast majority of the optimization (from N=14 to N=59). This suggests that the algorithm either converged to a deceptive local optimum or found a very flat region around the global optimum from which it struggled to extract further improvements, even with numerous evaluations.
3.  **Repeated Failure of Diverse Acquisition Functions:** Various acquisition functions, including information-theoretic ones (qPES, qJES, qKG, qMES), exploration-focused (PosSTD, UCB, TS), and exploitation-focused (EI, LogEI, PI, LogPI, PosMean), were employed, yet none consistently broke the `f_min` stagnation or fully stabilized the model.

These characteristics strongly point towards a **highly multi-modal objective function with a complex, potentially deceptive landscape, and many local optima.** The GP consistently lost its global understanding while trying to navigate these complexities, leading to the erratic lengthscale behavior and the inability to find a significantly better minimum.

A common benchmark function that exhibits such behavior, with a vast number of local minima, a flat outer region, and a deep, narrow global minimum, is the **Ackley function (or a scaled/shifted variant of it)**. Its "golf-course" like structure often causes surrogate models to struggle with both global exploration and fine-grained exploitation, leading to oscillations in model parameters and difficulties in locating the precise global optimum.
