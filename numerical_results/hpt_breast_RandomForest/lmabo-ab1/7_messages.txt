*   **Ackley Function**: Known for its exponential term creating a large central hole surrounded by many local minima due to cosine modulation, and a generally flat "outer" region. This combination of sharp local minima, a global minimum, and flatter regions can cause ARD lengthscales to fluctuate significantly as the GP tries to model these different behaviors.
*   **Rastrigin Function**: Characterized by many local minima created by a sum of quadratic and cosine terms. It's very easy to get stuck in one of these minima.

Both are strong candidates, but the extreme swings in lengthscale values (from small to thousands) might be more indicative of the Ackley function's structure where regions of strong oscillations blend into flatter areas. The consistent failure to improve `f_min` points to being trapped in a local minimum, which is a common challenge for the Ackley function.

My guess is the **Ackley Function** (possibly scaled and shifted to have negative values).
