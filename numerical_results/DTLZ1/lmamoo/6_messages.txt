    *   **Meaning of Large Lengthscales:** In a Gaussian Process, a very large lengthscale for an input dimension means the model perceives the objective function as varying very slowly or negligibly along that dimension within the observed data. Effectively, it suggests that dimension might be inactive or a "dummy variable" for that particular objective.
    *   **Meaning of Highly Variable Lengthscales:** A large standard deviation in lengthscales implies that the model is very uncertain about the true influence of that dimension, or that the influence is complex/non-stationary, or that it's struggling to determine if the variable is active or inactive.

2.  **Inactive/Dummy Variables:** The DTLZ (Deb, Thiele, Laumanns, Zitzler) test problems are specifically designed with a concept of "inactive variables" (often denoted as `k` parameters). For these functions, some input dimensions (`x_i` for `i <= m-1+k`) contribute to the objectives through a common "g" function, while others (`x_i` for `i > m-1+k`) only affect the "g" function and not the initial objective calculations. This often leads to GP models struggling to learn the relevance of these inactive dimensions, manifesting as very large or highly uncertain lengthscales.

3.  **Dimensionality and Objectives:** With D=10 input dimensions and J=4 objectives, this setup is common for DTLZ problems, which are often used to benchmark algorithms on problems with varying numbers of objectives and decision variables, including those with inactive variables.
