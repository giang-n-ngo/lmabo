**Justification:**

1.  **Multi-modality and Local Minima Traps:** The continuous struggle to improve `f_min` and the frequent getting stuck (indicated by small `shortest_distance` and unchanged `f_min` for many iterations) are classic symptoms of a highly multi-modal function, where the optimizer gets trapped in local optima.
2.  **Fluctuating Lengthscales:** The Ackley function is known for its "egg crate" like structure, featuring a central deep basin (global minimum) surrounded by a large number of regularly spaced local minima, and often a relatively flat outer region. This structure would cause a Gaussian Process to struggle with learning consistent lengthscales: very small lengthscales would be needed to capture the oscillations and wiggliness around the local minima, while very large lengthscales might be fitted in the flatter, smoother regions, leading to the observed dramatic shifts in the model's perception of smoothness and relevance of dimensions.
3.  **D=3:** The function is typically defined in multiple dimensions, and D=3 is a common test dimensionality.
4.  **f_min values:** While the standard Ackley function has a global minimum of 0 (at 0,0,...0), it is very common in optimization testing to scale and shift these benchmark functions. The observed `f_min` values of -0.945 and -0.949 are plausible for a scaled and negated version of the Ackley function (e.g., minimizing `-(Ackley(x) + C)`).

The constant need to switch between exploitation and robust exploration (like TS and UCB) to simply stabilize the model, rather than finding consistent improvement, reinforces the idea of a complex, multi-modal landscape where the GP has difficulty building a globally consistent surrogate.
