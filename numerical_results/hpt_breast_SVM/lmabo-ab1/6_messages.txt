**Justification for Schwefel Function:**
The Schwefel function is notoriously difficult for optimization algorithms due to its **deceptive nature**. It features a global minimum that is very deep but is surrounded by large, relatively flat basins (local minima) that are far from the global minimum. This setup often leads optimizers to get trapped in these deceptive local minima, resulting in:
*   **Prolonged stagnation:** The optimizer spends a long time in a flat basin of attraction, just as observed.
*   **Small, hard-to-find improvements:** Escaping these deceptive basins or pinpointing the true minimum requires significant exploration and can yield only incremental gains initially.
*   **High model uncertainty and anisotropic behavior:** The GP model would naturally struggle to accurately map such a highly non-convex, deceptive, and rugged landscape, leading to the volatile lengthscales and high outputscale observed.

The observed behavior perfectly aligns with the challenges presented by the Schwefel function.
