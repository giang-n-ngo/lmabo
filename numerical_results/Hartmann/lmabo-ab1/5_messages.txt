This optimization process strongly suggests that the objective function has **many local minima**.

The most telling characteristic is the repeated cycle:
1.  An acquisition function (often an exploitation-heavy one like `PosMean`, `EI`, or `PI`) successfully finds a new `f_min`.
2.  Immediately, the "shortest distance" metric drops to an extremely small value (often below 0.1, sometimes even below 0.05). This indicates severe over-exploitation, where the optimizer is sampling very close to the newly found local minimum.
3.  Despite high model confidence (consistently low and stable lengthscales in later stages), the `f_min` then stagnates.
4.  To break this stagnation and over-exploitation, exploration-focused or information-theoretic acquisition functions (`TS`, `UCB`, `qMES`, `qPES`, `qKG`) are used. These functions typically increase the "shortest distance" and eventually lead to the discovery of another, slightly better local minimum, restarting the cycle.

This pattern of "find a local minimum -> get stuck and over-exploit -> explore to jump to another local minimum -> find another slightly better local minimum" is a classic behavior on a **rugged, multi-modal landscape with many local optima**. The fact that `f_min` consistently improves over time suggests these local minima are progressively deeper, converging towards a global minimum.

Such functions are commonly used as benchmark problems in optimization, with **Rastrigin** and **Ackley** being prime examples that exhibit this kind of behavior. Given the consistent local smoothness (low lengthscales) but global ruggedness, a function like **Rastrigin** is a strong candidate.
