Based on the optimization behavior and model characteristics, it's highly likely this is a challenging multi-objective optimization problem characterized by:

1.  **Complex, potentially multi-modal objective landscapes:** The wide range of lengthscales and outputscales across objectives (e.g., Obj1 lengthscales ranging from 1.4 to nearly 6000, Obj2 outputscale over 5000, Obj4 outputscale near 3) indicates highly heterogeneous sensitivities to input parameters and varying magnitudes of variation, making the landscape rugged.
2.  **A Pareto front with complex geometry or many local Pareto fronts:** The frequent drops in "shortest distance" to critically low values, coupled with `qNEHVI` and `qLogNEHVI` consistently making small, local hypervolume gains, suggests that the optimizer is effectively exploiting local Pareto regions.
3.  **Difficulty in global exploration:** The consistent failure of "exploratory" acquisition functions (`qMOPES`, `qLBMOMES`, `qHVKG`, `qLBMOJES`, `qParEGO`) to improve hypervolume, despite sometimes increasing the shortest distance, implies that merely sampling new or uncertain regions doesn't readily lead to finding genuinely better, distant Pareto fronts. This could indicate a highly deceptive landscape or disconnected Pareto fronts that are hard to jump between.

Given these characteristics, especially the combination of 7 dimensions and 4 objectives, and the observed "local exploitation traps" vs. "exploration struggles", this problem strongly resembles a variant from the **DTLZ (Deb, Thiele, Laumanns, Zitzler) benchmark family**.

Specifically, I would guess it's a variant of **DTLZ7**, which is known for having a **disconnected Pareto front**. This characteristic perfectly explains why local improvements are easy (via `qNEHVI`/`qLogNEHVI`) but discovering entirely new, better parts of the Pareto front (which would require significant exploration to "jump" across gaps) is very difficult for the attempted exploratory acquisition functions. The objectives being negative and having differing scales is also common with shifted/scaled benchmark problems.

**My guess is: DTLZ7 (or a similarly challenging DTLZ variant, potentially with modifications to scale/shift objectives or introduce further ruggedness).**
