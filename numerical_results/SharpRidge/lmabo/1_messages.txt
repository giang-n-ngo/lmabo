This was almost certainly a **multi-modal function**, characterized by numerous local minima.

The strong evidence for this conclusion includes:
1.  **Repeated Stalling and Escapes:** The optimization repeatedly got stuck at specific `f_min` values (e.g., 669.292, 458.538, 133.731, 121.503, 118.233) for multiple iterations, indicating it found local minima. The eventual (and often hard-won) breakthroughs to lower `f_min` values demonstrate the presence of deeper minima that required strategic exploration to discover.
2.  **Lengthscale Behavior:** The initial high variability and large mean of lengthscales, followed by their significant reduction and stabilization once a local minimum was found, suggests the model quickly became confident about the local landscape but initially struggled with the global structure.
3.  **Acquisition Function Cycling:** The necessity to frequently switch between purely exploitative functions (EI, PI, PosMean), information-theoretic functions (qPES, qMES, qJES), and stochastic exploration (TS) highlights the challenge of navigating a landscape with deceptive local optima. If it were unimodal, a more consistent application of EI or LogEI would likely suffice.

Classic examples of such functions include **Ackley**, **Rastrigin**, or **Griewank** functions, which are frequently used to benchmark optimization algorithms precisely because of their multi-modal nature and the challenge they pose in finding the true global minimum.
