The lengthscales remained consistently small and stable (e.g., [0.442, 0.819], Std Dev 0.159), and the outputscale also steadily decreased, signifying the GP accurately models a very smooth and relatively flat region. The `shortest distance` frequently became critically small (e.g., 0.0109), indicating intense local exploitation.

4.  **Failure of Diverse Strategies at Plateau:** Even after trying a wide array of acquisition functions, including sophisticated information-theoretic methods (qPES, qMES, qJES), stochastic methods (TS), and balanced exploration/exploitation (UCB), the `f_min` did not budge for extended periods. This suggests that either the true global minimum has been found and it's extremely flat at the bottom, or the search is trapped in a very good local minimum from which it cannot escape because the model, despite its local confidence, is not receiving enough signal to explore further globally.

Combining these characteristics, the function behaves like an **Ackley function**. The Ackley function has a broad, roughly flat region with small ripples, which could account for the initial high uncertainty. It then possesses a very deep and sharp global minimum, surrounded by many local minima. Once the optimizer enters the basin of the global minimum, the GP would quickly learn the local smoothness (small lengthscales) and exploit it, leading to the rapid `f_min` improvements. The final, persistent plateau, despite high local model confidence and diverse acquisition attempts, would be due to the extreme sharpness or flatness right at the global optimum, making further, minute improvements extremely difficult to pinpoint with a Gaussian Process.
