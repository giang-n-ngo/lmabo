Based on the repeated patterns in your optimization log, I would guess this is the **Rosenbrock function**.

Here's why:
1.  **Consistent `f_min: -0.000`**: The objective function never improved below 0. This is a strong indicator that the global minimum of the function *is* 0 (or very close to it), and the optimizer struggled to find that exact point. The Rosenbrock function (in 2D) has a global minimum of 0.
2.  **Extreme Lengthscale Volatility**: The lengthscales repeatedly swung between very small (hundreds, tens) and extremely large (thousands, even 8000+). This behavior is highly characteristic of the Rosenbrock function, which has a very long, narrow, and *flat* parabolic valley leading to its global minimum. When the optimizer is exploring the flat parts of this valley, the GP model perceives the function as very smooth, leading to large lengthscales. When it finds curvature or "edges" of the valley, the lengthscales become smaller. The constant oscillation suggests the optimizer is repeatedly entering and struggling within this challenging valley.
3.  **Frequent Shortest Distance Clustering**: The "shortest distance" often became very small, indicating the optimizer was getting stuck in local regions or repeatedly sampling very close to previous points. This aligns with the difficulty of navigating the narrow Rosenbrock valley.
4.  **Acquisition Function Cycles**: The need to constantly switch between aggressive exploration (TS, PosSTD) and exploitation (EI, PI, LogEI) without improving `f_min` further underscores the difficulty of the landscape. The shifts to TS and PosSTD were often necessary to "reset" the search and break out of localized over-exploitation when the model was uncertain and stuck.

The combination of a global minimum at 0, extreme difficulty in precisely locating it, and the peculiar behavior of the GP lengthscales strongly points to the Rosenbrock function.
