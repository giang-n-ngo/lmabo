The Ackley function has a large, nearly flat outer region leading to a deep, multi-modal "valley" around its global minimum. During the optimization, the model likely got trapped in these relatively flat regions (e.g., at `f_min` -0.003 and then -0.439), where the actual function variance over the sampled points was so small that the GP's outputscale effectively collapsed to zero. This makes it incredibly difficult for standard acquisition functions to find a gradient for improvement, leading to prolonged stagnation until a sufficiently exploratory method (like TS, UCB, or information-theoretic ones) sampled a point that revealed the true, more variable landscape leading to deeper minima. The sudden increase in `outputscale` and large drops in `f_min` confirm the escape from these perceived flat regions.

This combination of multi-modality, a specific global minimum, and the challenging "flat plateau" behavior strongly points towards a shifted and scaled Ackley function.
