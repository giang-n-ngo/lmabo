3.  **Difficulty in Optimization (Stagnation):** The most compelling evidence is the prolonged stagnation of `f_min` at 0.060 after an initial significant drop. The Rosenbrock function is famously difficult to optimize due to its narrow, curved, and flat valley leading to the global minimum. Many acquisition functions, including those designed for exploration (PosSTD), exploitation (EI, PI, LogEI, LogPI, PosMean), and advanced information-theoretic methods (qKG, qPES, qMES, qJES), all failed to improve `f_min` after its initial discovery. This behavior is highly characteristic of the Rosenbrock's challenging landscape, where it's easy to find the general region of the minimum but extremely hard to pinpoint the exact minimum along the valley.
4.  **Thompson Sampling's Unique Success (and eventual stagnation):** Thompson Sampling was the only function that managed to improve `f_min` from 2.532 to 0.060. This highlights its robust exploration-exploitation balance which might have been crucial in finding the "mouth" of the Rosenbrock valley. However, even TS eventually got stuck in the same difficult region, reinforcing the inherent challenge of the function itself.
