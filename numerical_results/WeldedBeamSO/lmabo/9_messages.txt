This indicates that the algorithm was frequently sampling points extremely close to already evaluated ones, often due to over-exploitation within a local basin.
3.  **Heavy Reliance on Pure Exploration (`PosSTD`)**: I repeatedly resorted to `PosSTD` in the later stages (N=16, 22, 23, 38, 39, 40, 41, 48, 49, 50, 53, 54, 55, 56, 57, 58). This aggressive pure exploration was necessary to try and force the algorithm out of these perceived local traps and encourage sampling in entirely new regions, explicitly disregarding the immediate expected improvement. This pattern is characteristic of a landscape with many deceptive local minima.
4.  **Cycling Through Exploration/Exploitation Functions**: The need to switch between exploitation-focused (EI, LogEI, qKG) and exploration-focused (TS, qPES, qMES, UCB) acquisition functions, often followed by a rapid return to `PosSTD`, further underscores the complex and multimodal nature of the objective function.

In summary, the optimization behavior strongly points towards an objective function that is challenging due to its **many local minima**, making it easy for the optimizer to get stuck and difficult to guarantee finding the global optimum within a limited budget. Classic benchmark functions like **Ackley, Rastrigin, or Levy functions** often exhibit such characteristics.
