The final `f_min` of -8.800 is a deep negative value. The Michalewicz function is known for being highly multimodal with many local minima, and its global minimum typically becomes deeper (more negative) with increasing dimensionality. For 8 dimensions, the standard Michalewicz function's global minimum is approximately -7.957, so achieving -8.800 suggests either a slight modification (e.g., a different 'm' parameter in its definition) or a different, but structurally similar, function.

2.  **Prolonged Stagnation:** The most striking feature is the extremely long period of stagnation at `f_min = -8.800` (36 consecutive iterations) despite a wide array of acquisition functions being used (exploratory, exploitative, information-theoretic). This behavior is characteristic of functions where the global optimum is very narrow, sharp, or lies in a highly localized region that is exceedingly difficult for the Bayesian Optimization process (even with a well-tuned GP) to precisely pinpoint and exploit, even when it's "close."

3.  **Volatile Lengthscales:** The lengthscales fluctuated significantly throughout the optimization, sometimes converging dramatically (indicating local model confidence) and other times spreading out widely (indicating high uncertainty). This volatility is consistent with an anisotropic and complex landscape, possibly with "ridges" or "valleys" of varying widths and orientations, making it challenging for the ARD kernel to consistently model the function's smoothness and parameter relevance globally. 4.  **Dimensionality (D=8):** While 8 dimensions is not exceptionally high, it's sufficient to create a complex search space where multimodal functions become very challenging to optimize effectively.
