1.  **High Dimensionality (D=8):** Ackley is commonly used for higher-dimensional black-box optimization benchmarks.
2.  **Initial Complexity & Multimodality:** The early wide range and high variability in lengthscales, coupled with the initial exploration (qMES, qPES, PosSTD, UCB), strongly suggest a highly multimodal landscape with many local minima. Ackley is notorious for this.
3.  **Convergence to a Rugged, Localized Minimum:** As the optimization progressed (especially from N=34 onwards), the lengthscales consistently became much smaller and less varied, indicating that the GP model successfully localized a region of interest. The small lengthscales imply that within this region, the function is quite rugged or "wiggly." This is a hallmark of Ackley, which has a global minimum that is deep but very narrow, surrounded by many quickly varying local minima.
4.  **Deep Global Minimum with a "Flat" Plateau:** The objective value quickly improved to -8.500 and then to -8.800. However, the optimizer then remained stuck at -8.800 for a significant number of iterations (from N=35 to N=66), despite employing various acquisition functions (including aggressive exploitation strategies like EI, LogEI, and PosMean). This indicates either:
    *   The true global minimum is exactly -8.800 (or incredibly close), and the optimizer converged.
    *   The function is extremely flat or has an extremely narrow, precise optimum around -8.800, making it very difficult for the optimizer to find a *significantly* better point. This "difficulty in improvement" even with precise models is a classic challenge with Ackley, where getting closer to the true minimum (often 0, but shifted here to -8.800) becomes exponentially harder.
