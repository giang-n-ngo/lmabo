This behavior strongly suggests that the objective function is **the Ackley function**.

The Ackley function is known for:
*   **A sharp, deep global minimum** (often at (0,0,...,0)) surrounded by many local minima.
*   **An almost flat "outer" region** at higher values, making it difficult for optimizers to locate the global minimum from far away.
*   **Deceptive local minima:** Once an optimizer falls into a local minimum, it can be very difficult to escape and find the true global minimum, especially if the local minimum is close to the global one or the landscape is very flat around it.

The observed pattern of quickly finding a good minimum (like falling into a good local minimum near the global one), then becoming completely stuck and repeatedly sampling the same or very similar points, perfectly aligns with the challenges presented by the Ackley function. The optimizer appears to have converged to a very good local minimum (or possibly the global minimum itself, if -9.210 is the true global value for this specific scaled/shifted version) and could not find any further improvement due to the function's rugged and deceptive landscape.
